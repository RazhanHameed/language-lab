# Zazaki for Kurmanji speakers

A grammar sketch of Zazaki (Kirmanckî / Zazakî) for someone who already speaks Kurmanji. It is the reference for the `kmr>zza` bridge course (`js/bridge/zza/`).

Every Zazaki example below is copied from a published source and cited. None is made up. The only exceptions are a few forms combined from attested pieces by a rule Lezgîn states; §16 lists them. Kurmanji glosses are in Hawar standard.

The main source throughout is **Lezgîn 2013**: Roşan Lezgîn, *Ji bo Kurmancan bi Awayê Muqayeseyî Gramera Kirdkî (Zazakî)* ("A comparative Kirdkî/Zazaki grammar for Kurmanji speakers"), Weşanxaneya Roşna, Diyarbekir 2013. The book is written in Kurmanji and puts a Kurmanji sentence next to every Zazaki one, so it is written for exactly this reader. Section numbers (§) below refer to that book.

## Bi kurtî (kurmancî)

Zazakî (kirdkî, kirmanckî, dimilkî) ji bo kurmancîaxivan nêzîk e. Gelek tişt wek hev in: dema borî ya ergatîv (kirde tewandî, lêker li gor bireserê), pêşgirên `bi-` û `me-`, rêza peyvan, û gelek peyv. Lê pênc cudahiyên mezin hene:

1. **Zayend li ser navdêrê bi xwe xuya ye.** `dar` (nêr) darê hişk e, `dare` (mê) dara şîn e. Pirhejmar bi `-î` ye: `darî`.
2. **Lêker û lêkera nîvco li gor zayendê diguherin.** `O keno` (ew dike, nêr), `A kena` (ew dike, mê).
3. **Di dema niha de pêşgira `di-` tune.** `Ez kena` = Ez dikim.
4. **Gelek daçek paşdaçek in.** `Dîyarbekir ra` = ji Diyarbekirê, `kitab de` = di kitêbê de.
5. **`Ez` her tim mê tê hesibandin**, mêr bibêje jî: `Ez ameya` = Ez hatim.

## 0. The language, its names and its place

Speakers call the language by different names in different regions (Lezgîn 2013, §1.6):
- **Kirdkî**: people call themselves *kird* in Diyarbekir, Çewlîg and part of Xarpêt.
- **Kirmanckî**: *kirmanc* in Dêrsim, Erzingan and Mûş.
- **Dimilkî**: *dimilî* in Çêrmûg, Sêwreg, Aldûş and Motkan.
- **Zazakî**: *zaza* in part of Xarpêt.

Lezgîn says *kird* and *kirmanc* are ethnic names and *dimilî* and *zaza* are tribal names. He uses *kirdkî* because most speakers do.

There are two main varieties (§1.7):
- the Dêrsim variety;
- the variety outside Dêrsim.

Dêrsim says `Ma nonê xo weme` where the rest say `Ma nanê xwu wenê` ("Em nanê xwe dixwin"). Village dialects differ a lot, mainly in pronunciation (§1.8). The foreword says Zazaki is spoken only in Turkey, and that pressure to shift to Turkish has been even heavier on it than on Kurmanji. This sketch follows the standard written language that Lezgîn uses. It is close to the spelling standard of the Vate working group.

**On classification.** Most Zazaki speakers consider themselves Kurds and their language a form of Kurdish. Lezgîn 2013 (§1.5) lists *kirdkî* as one of five branches of Kurdish, beside Kurmanji, Sorani, Hewramî (Goranî) and Lurî. The book itself shows how close the two are: one grammar of ergative past tenses, cases and preverbs serves both, and very often only the sound shape of a word differs.

Many linguists, looking at the history of sounds and grammar, group Zazaki with Gorani/Hawrami in a Gorani–Zaza branch of Northwest Iranian, separate from the Kurmanji–Sorani–Southern Kurdish group. Examples are D. N. MacKenzie's work on the origins of Kurdish and Ludwig Paul's *Zazaki: Grammatik und Versuch einer Dialektologie* (1998). This sketch does not cite these works line by line. They are named only to show that this view exists.

The two views are not about ranking the languages: both describe close sister varieties that have lived side by side for centuries. This course treats Zazaki as a sister of Kurmanji. Speaker estimates vary widely, and none of our sources gives a figure, so we give none.

## 1. Sounds and letters

Zazaki uses the same 31-letter Latin alphabet as Kurmanji (§2.1–2.2). The letters you need to watch:

| letter | in Zazaki | Kurmanji comparison | example (source) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `i` / `î` | short `i` vs long `î`, as in Kurmanji. Lezgîn contrasts `bira` 'brother' and `bîra` 'well' (§2.5). | same letters, same values | `Min û birayê xo me awa bîrî şimite.` = Min û birayê xwe ava bîrê vexwar. (§2.5) |
| `ê` | as in Kurmanji `belê`, `êvar` | same | `kêran` (§2.2) |
| `rr` | a heavy (trilled) r; single `r` is light | Kurmanji also has rr, but Zazaki always writes it | `kerra` 'stone', `serre` 'year', `zerrî` 'heart' (§2.6) |
| `w` / `v` | two different sounds | same; Zazaki `w` often answers Kurmanji `v` (§11) | `vewre` 'snow' has both (§2.5) |
| `q`, `x`, `h` | as in Kurmanji | same | `qalin, qirik` (§2.5) |

**Stress matters more than in Kurmanji** (§3.7). Feminine and plural nouns are stressed on the *first* syllable. Masculine nouns are stressed on the *last*. So `gule` 'rose' (f) and `gule` 'bullet' (m) are spelled the same and told apart only by stress.

## 2. Spelling convention used in this course

We write Zazaki as Lezgîn 2013 does, with the Kurdish letters a Kurmanji reader already knows:
- short `i`, long `î`, and no dotless `ı`;
- `îy` before a vowel: `Dîyarbekir`, `nîyo`, `bîyo`;
- `rr` for the heavy r;
- the feminine ending `-e` always written;
- the forms `-an`, `-am` and `j`, where village speech varies (`-on/-ûn`, `-om`, `c/z`). Lezgîn follows the Vate group's choice here (§19).

Most Zazaki online (Zazaki Wikipedia, Zazaki Wiktionary, Vate books) uses a Turkish-keyboard spelling. When we take a form from such a source, we change only the letters:

| you will see online | we write | examples |
|---|---|---|
| `ı` | `i` | zıwan → ziwan, kıtab → kitab, merdım → merdim |
| `iy` | `îy` | niyo → nîyo, Diyarbekır → Dîyarbekir |
| open-syllable `i` (long) | `î` | tiya → tîya, cıni → cinî |

We change spellings, never words. Dialect forms such as `keyna` for `kêna` 'girl', `çeye` for `keye` 'house' and `roc` for `roj` 'day' are kept apart as variants.

## 3. Pronouns

**Personal pronouns** (Lezgîn 2013, §6.1)

| | direct | oblique | Kurmanji |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | ez | mi | ez / min |
| you (sg) | ti | to | tu / te |
| he, it (m) | o | ey | ew / wî |
| she, it (f) | a | aye | ew / wê |
| we | ma | ma | em / me |
| you (pl) | şima | şima | hûn / we |
| they | ê | înan | ew / wan |

What is different from Kurmanji:
- **Third person has gender**, as in Kurmanji `wî / wê`, but here also in the *direct* case: `o` (m) and `a` (f).
- **`ma` and `şima` never change.** Kurmanji has `em/me` and `hûn/we`; Zazaki uses one form for both cases (§6.1, §18.1).
- **`ez` is always grammatically feminine**, whoever is speaking, so its verbs take feminine endings (§6.1). A man also says `Ez ameya` 'I came' and `Ez baş a` 'I am fine'.

**Demonstratives** (§5.2.2.1, §6.2): this is gendered too.

| | this, direct | this, oblique | that, direct | that, oblique |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| m | no | ney | o | ey |
| f | na | naye | a | aye |
| pl | nê | nînan | ê | înan |
| Kurmanji | ev | vî / vê / van | ew | wî / wê / wan |

Examples:
- `No ney ra weşikêr o.` = Ev ji vî xweşiktir e.
- `Nê nînan ra kêrameyêr ê.` = Ev ji van bikêrhatîtir in. (§6.2)

Other pronouns:
- reflexive `xo` = xwe (`A xo ra hes kena.` = Ew ji xwe hez dike);
- `ci` = jê (`Ez ci ra hes kena.` = Ez jê hez dikim);
- possessive `yê`, which never changes: `O, yê min o. / A, yê min a. / Ê, yê min ê.` = Ew yê / ya / yên min e/in (§6.3).

Question words (§6.4):
- `çi` = what (çi)
- `kam` = who (kî)
- `kamcîn` = which (kîjan)
- `senî` = how (çawa)
- `çira` = why (çima)
- `key` = when (kengî)
- `ça / kotî` = where (ku / li kû)

## 4. The copula ("to be")

As in Kurmanji, the present copula is a small word after the predicate. In Zazaki its form shows the **gender** of the subject.

**Present tense**

| | positive | source | negative | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ez | `Ez baş a.` | §1 reading | `ez bawer nîya` | §9 |
| ti (m) | `Ti senîn î?` | §1 reading | `Ti mi ra dergêr nîyî.` | E–Z sentences |
| ti (f) | `ti yew fîl a.` | E–Z sentences | `ti uca de nîya.` | E–Z sentences |
| o | `O Roşan o.` | §16.6 | `O Roşan nîyo.` | §16.6 |
| a | `A Asmîn a.` | §16.6 | `A Asmîne nîya.` | §16.6 |
| ma | `Ma Kurdistan ra yê.` | §4.5 | `Ma merdim nîyê.` | E–Z sentences |
| şima | `Şima kam ê?` | E–Z sentences | `Şima ... nîyê!` | E–Z sentences |
| ê | `Ê malim ê.` | §16.6 | `Ê malim nîyê.` | §16.6 |
| Kurmanji | im, î, e/ye, in/ne | | ne ... im/î/e/in | |

"Source" is Lezgîn 2013 unless it says "E–Z sentences" (see Sources).

How it works:
- **Endings:** `a` for 1sg and for feminine, `î` for 2sg masculine, `o` for masculine, `ê` for plural.
- **After a vowel** the copula takes a `y`, as Kurmanji `ye` does: `Nameyê aye Roşna ya.` = Navê wê Roşna ye. `Nameyê aye çi yo?` = Navê wê çi ye? (§3.6)
- **Negative:** `nîyo / nîya / nîyê`, plus `nîyî` for 2sg masculine.
- **Noun endings and the copula:** a positive copula drops the feminine `-e` or plural `-î` of the noun before it. The negative keeps them (§3.6, §16.6):
  - `A gul a.` / `A gule nîya.` = Ew gul e. / Ew ne gul e.
  - `Ê zimbêl ê.` / `Ê zimbêlî nîyê.` = Ew simbêl in. / Ew ne simbêl in.

**Past ("was"), from `bîyene`** (§16.7, plus texts in the book)

| | form | source |
|---|---|---|
| ez | `Ez tena keye de bîya.` = Ez bi tenê li mal bûm. | §17 reading text |
| o | `O Roşan bi.` / `O Roşan nêbi.` | §16.7 |
| a | `A Asmîne bî.` / `A Asmîne nêbî.` | §16.7 |
| ma | `ma se way û bira bî` = em wek xwişk û bira bûn | §17 reading text |
| ê | `Ê malimî bî.` / `Ê malimî nêbî.` | §16.7 |

The 2sg and 2pl past copula do not appear in our sources.

## 5. "There is" and "to have"

Zazaki has an existence verb that agrees with the thing that exists: `esto` (m), `esta` (f), `estê` (pl). The negative is `çin o / çin a / çin ê`. Kurmanji has `heye / tune` with no gender.
- `çiqas werdo weş dinya de esto` = çiqas xwarinên xweş li dinyayê hene (§5 reading text)
- `aye ra başêr esta` = ji wê baştir heye (§6.2)
- `Tayê merdimî estê` = Hinek meriv hene (§6 reading text)
- `Karê ma bi filan û bêvanan çin o!` = Karê me bi filan û bêvanan tune ye! (§5.2.2.4)
- `De vaje, çi esto çi çin o?` = De bêje, çi heye çi tune ye? (§11)

The past forms are `estbi / estibi` (m), `estibî` (f) and `estbîy` (pl).

**"To have"** is built like Kurmanji `min ... heye`: the possessor goes in a linking phrase, and the verb agrees with the thing owned.
- `Yew kelceyê mi estibi.` = Min gîskek hebû. (§8 reading text)
- `Hişê mi zî esto!` = I have a mind too! (E–Z sentences)

## 6. Verbs

### 6.1 Infinitive and stems

Zazaki infinitives end in `-ene / -yene` where Kurmanji has `-in / -în` (§15, §16.1):
- `kerdene` kirin, `berdene` birin, `ardene` anîn, `kewtene` ketin
- `wendene` xwendin, `nuştene` nivîsîn, `vatene` gotin, `werdene` xwarin

Remove `-ene` and you have the past stem, just as removing `-in` does in Kurmanji (glossary to the book): `kerd-` = kir-, `kewt-` = ket-.

The present stem is often different, as in Kurmanji (§16.1):

| past | present | | Kurmanji past | Kurmanji present |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| vat- | van- | say | got- | bêj- |
| nuşt- | nus- | write | nivîsî- | nivîs- |
| rot- | roş- | sell | firot- | firoş- |
| vist- | fîn- | hit, put | xist- | xîn- |
| girewt- | gên- | take | girt- | gir- |
| waşt- | waz- | want | xwest- | xwaz- |
| eşt- | erz- | throw | avêt- | avêj- |
| werişt- | werz- | get up | rabû- | rabi- |

Zazaki also has a second verbal noun in `-iş` (`kerdiş` 'doing', `wendiş` 'reading'), and these are always masculine (§15.1).

### 6.2 Present tense: no `di-`, and the ending shows gender

Kurmanji marks the present with `di-`. Zazaki has no prefix. Instead it adds `-en-` and a person-and-gender ending.

**Present tense** (Lezgîn 2013, §17.1.1)

| | kewtene 'fall' | kerdene 'do' | şîyene 'go' | negative | Kurmanji |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ez | kewena | kena | şina | nêkena | dikevim / dikim / diçim |
| ti (m) | kewenî | kenî | şinî | nêkenî | dikevî |
| ti (f) | kewena | kena | şina | nêkena | dikevî |
| o | keweno | keno | şino | nêkeno | dikeve |
| a | kewena | kena | şina | nêkena | dikeve |
| ma | kewenê | kenê | şinê | nêkenê | dikevin |
| şima | kewenê | kenê | şinê | nêkenê | dikevin |
| ê | kewenê | kenê | şinê | nêkenê | dikevin |

The endings are:
- `-a` for 1sg and for 2sg and 3sg feminine;
- `-î` for 2sg masculine;
- `-o` for 3sg masculine;
- `-ê` for all plurals.

Kurmanji only tells singular from plural. Zazaki also tells masculine from feminine.

Examples (§3.2, §4.1):
- `Goliko sûr yeno.` / `Golika sûre yena.` / `Golikê sûrî yenê.` = Golikê sor tê. / Golika sor tê. / Golikên sor tên.
- `Ehmed nan weno.` / `Zelale nan wena.` / `Wendekarî nan wenê.` = Ehmed / Zelal nan dixwe; Xwendekar nan dixwin.

The negative prefix is `nê-`, where Kurmanji has `na-`: `nêkeno` = nake, `Ez nêzana` = Ez nizanim.

**"Right now" (progressive)** uses `ha`, much like Kurmanji `va / wa ye` (§11, §17.1.2):
- `Ehmed ha yo nuseno.` = Ehmed niha dinivîse.
- `Asmîne ha ya nusena.` = Asmîn niha dinivîse.
- The short form is `Ehmed ha keweno.`

In the present the subject is direct and the object oblique, as in Kurmanji: `Ez ey vînena.` = Ez wî dibînim (§16.4). A definite masculine object takes `-î`: `Ehmed goştî roşeno` = Ehmed goştî difiroşe. An indefinite one does not: `Ehmed goşt roşeno` (§4.5).

### 6.3 Past of intransitive verbs

The subject is direct and the verb agrees with it, as in Kurmanji, but again with gender.

**Past of intransitive verbs** (Lezgîn 2013, §17.2.1–17.2.3)

| | simple past | negative | perfect ("has fallen") | pluperfect | Kurmanji |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ez | kewta | nêkewta | kewta | kewtibîya | ketim / ketime / ketibûm |
| ti (m) | kewtî | nêkewtî | kewtê | kewtibî | ketî |
| ti (f) | kewta | nêkewta | kewta | kewtibîya | ketî |
| o | kewt | nêkewt | kewto | kewtibi | ket / ketiye / ketibû |
| a | kewte | nêkewte | kewta | kewtibî | ket |
| ma, şima, ê | kewtî | nêkewtî | kewtê | kewtibî | ketin / ketine / ketibûn |

The same pattern with 'come' and 'go' (§18.1.1):
- `Ez ameya û şîya.` = Ez hatim û çûm (also when the speaker is a man);
- `Ti ameyî û şîyî.` for a man and `Ti ameya û şîya.` for a woman;
- `O ameyo û şîyo.`, `Ma ameyê û şîyê.`

### 6.4 Past of transitive verbs: ergative, as in Kurmanji, plus gender

As in Kurmanji, the agent of a transitive verb in the past is **oblique**, the object is **direct**, and the verb agrees with the **object** (§16.3–16.4). The new thing is that the verb also agrees with the object's **gender**: masculine has no ending, feminine `-e`, plural `-î`.

**Past transitive, noun object: werdene 'eat'** (§16.4)

| agent | object m `nan` | object f `nane` | object pl `nanî` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ehmedî | nan werd | nane werde | nanî werdî |
| Zelale | nan werd | nane werde | nanî werdî |
| Wendekaran | nan werd | nane werde | nanî werdî |
| Kurmanji | nan xwar | nan xwar | nan xwarin |

More examples:
- `Ey saye werde.` = Wî sêv xwar. (`saye` 'apple' is feminine, so `-e`.)
- `Mi kitabî wendî.` = Min kitêb xwendin.
- `Înan awe şimite.` = Wan av vexwar.

**Past transitive, pronoun object: dîyene 'see'** (§16.4)

| | Zazaki | Kurmanji |
|---|---|---|
| I saw you (m) | `Mi ti dî.` | Min tu dîtî. |
| I saw you (f) | `Mi ti dîye.` | Min tu dîtî. |
| I saw her | `Mi a dîye.` | Min ew dît. |
| I saw them | `Mi ê dîyî.` | Min ew dîtin. |
| he saw me | `Ey ez dîya.` (ez is feminine) | Wî ez dîtim. |

The oblique markers on the agent:
- masculine nouns `-î` (`Ehmedî`), and proper names always take it;
- feminine nouns keep `-e` and take nothing more (`Zelale`), where Kurmanji has `Zelalê`;
- plurals `-an` (`wendekaran`).

Within one sentence you switch pronoun sets exactly as in Kurmanji: `Ez ameya la mi nêdî.` = Ez hatim lê min nedît. (§18.1.1)

Lezgîn also gives full grids for `vistene` 'hit' with every pronoun as agent and object, in all three past tenses (§17.2):
- simple past: `Mi o vist / Mi a viste / Mi ê vistî`;
- perfect: `visto / vista / vistê`;
- pluperfect: `vistibi / vistibî`.

### 6.5 Past progressive and habitual (`-êne`): never changes

Kurmanji `di-` + past (`diket, dixwar`) becomes the past stem + `-êne` in Zazaki, the **same for every person and gender** (§17.1.3):
- `Ez / Ti / O / A / Ma / Şima / Ê kewtêne.` = diketim / diketî / diket / diketin.
- `Mi saye werdêne.` = Min sêv dixwar.

This is the easiest tense in Zazaki.

### 6.6 Future and subjunctive

The future is the particle `do` + `bi-` + present stem + subjunctive ending. Kurmanji uses `ê / dê` + `bi-` (§11, §17.3).

**Future** (Lezgîn 2013, §17.3.1, §18.1.1)

| | kewtene | şîyene | ameyene 'come' | Kurmanji |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ez | do bikewî | do şirî | do biyera | ez ê bikevim |
| ti | do bikewî | do şirî | do biyerî (m) / biyera (f) | tu yê bikevî |
| o | do bikewo | do şiro | do biyero | ew ê bikeve |
| a | do bikewo / bikewa | do şiro / şira | do biyera | ew ê bikeve |
| ma | do bikewê / bikewin | do şirê / şirin | do biyerê | em ê bikevin |
| şima | do bikewê | do şirê | do biyerê | hûn ê bikevin |
| ê | do bikewê | do şirê | do biyerê | ew ê bikevin |

Notes:
- **Negative** drops `bi-`: `do nêkewo` = dê nekeve.
- **Transitive future** works like the present, with the agent direct and the object oblique: `Ez do saye biberî.` = Ez ê sêvê bibim. `O do sayan bibero.` = Ew ê sêvan bibe.
- **1sg subjunctive has two forms.** Lezgîn's tables give `-î` (`Ez do binusî` = Ez ê binivîsim). His connected texts use `-a` after *ganî* 'must' and *ke* 'that' (`Ganî ez binusa û biba nuştox` = Divê ez binivîsim û bibim nivîskar), and so do our English–Zazaki sentences. See §13.

### 6.7 Imperative and negation

**Imperative** is `bi-` + present stem + `-e` for one person, `-ê` / `-în` for more, as in Kurmanji `bi-`:
- `Dersa xo biwane.` = Dersa xwe bixwîne.
- `Karê xo bike û nanê xo biwere.` = Karê xwe bike û nanê xwe bixwe.
- Plural: `Kurdênê, xo ver ro bidîn!` = Kurdino, li ber xwe bidin!

Irregular imperatives:

| Zazaki | Kurmanji | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `şo` | here / biçe | go |
| `werze` | rabe | get up |
| `bîya` | bîne | bring |
| `biyere` | were | come |
| `vaje` | bêje | say |

These come from §5, §8 and the reading text after §7.

**Negative imperative** is `me-`, the same as Kurmanji: `Karê xirabî meke!` = Karê xirab neke! `Meşo verê berê înan!` (§16.10).

**Summary of negation** (§16.6, §17)

| | Zazaki | Kurmanji |
|---|---|---|
| present | `nê-` (`nêkeno`) | `na-` (`nake`) |
| past | `nê-` (`nêkewt`) | `ne-` (`neket`) |
| imperative | `me-` | `me-` |
| copula | `nîyo / nîya / nîyê` | `ne ... e` |
| existence | `çin o / çin a / çin ê` | `tune` |

## 7. Nouns: gender, number, case, definiteness

### 7.1 Gender is visible on the noun itself

This is the biggest difference (§3.2–3.5). In Kurmanji you see a noun's gender only when it takes the linking particle or a case ending. In Zazaki you see it on the bare noun:
- **consonant at the end = masculine:** `adir` (agir), `nan`, `çem`, `dest`, `kitab` (§3.3);
- **`-e` at the end = feminine:** `awe` (av), `dare` (dar), `bize` (bizin), `roje` (roj), `şewe` (şev), `saye` (sêv), `qeleme` (qelem) (§3.4);
- **`-î` at the end = plural (direct case):** `kurdî`, `golikî`, `darî` (§3.5).

**One noun, three forms** (§3.2, §4)

| Zazaki | Kurmanji | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| golik (m) / golike (f) / golikî (pl) | golik | calf |
| dar (m) / dare (f) / darî (pl) | dar | dry wood / living tree / trees or timber |
| xal (m) / xale (f) / xalî (pl) | xal / xaltî | mother's brother / mother's sister |

There are exceptions:
- **masculine nouns ending in a vowel:** `bira`, `ca` 'place', `ga`, `va` 'wind', `zama` 'groom', `rî` 'face', `ko` 'mountain', `do` 'ayran', `estare` 'star', `gule` 'bullet' (§3.3);
- **feminine nouns ending in `-a` or `-î`:** `kêna` 'girl', `manga` 'cow', `dinya`, `gonî` 'blood', `kardî` 'knife' (§3.4).

Some genders differ from Kurmanji:
- `kitab`, `nimaj` (nimêj), `halîn` (hêlîn) and `estare` (stêr) are masculine in Zazaki and feminine in Kurmanji.
- Place names follow their form (`Dîyarbekir` m, `Riha` f). In Kurmanji every place name is feminine (§4).

### 7.2 Case

**Case** (Lezgîn 2013, §4.1–4.5)

| | direct | oblique |
|---|---|---|
| masculine, definite | `golik` | `golikî` (`-î`) |
| masculine, indefinite or generic | `goşt` | `goşt` (no ending) |
| feminine | `golike` | `golike` (keeps `-e`, nothing added) |
| plural | `golikî` | `golikan` (`-an`) |
| Kurmanji | golik / golik / golik | golikî / golikê / golikan |

Examples:
- `Qeleme bide Ehmedî.` = Qelemê bide Ehmed. `Kitabî bide Zelale.` = Kitêbê bide Zelalê. (§4.2)
- `Ez Dêrsim ra hes kena` is about the city and `Ez Dêrsimî ra hes kena` is about a man called Dêrsim. People's names always take `-î`; things take it only when definite (§4.5).

### 7.3 Indefinite

`-êk` (Kurmanji `-ek`), or `yew` 'one' before the noun, but never both (§5.2.2.4):
- `Bi yew qelema sûre nuşto.` or `Bi qelemêka sûre nuşto.` = Bi qelemeke sor nivîsiye.
- Not `yew qelemêka`.

With an adjective the indefinite noun takes `-êko` (m) or `-êka` (f): `Camêrdêko derg ame.` = Mêrekî dirêj hat. (Lezgîn prints `Yew camêrd/camêrdêko derg ame.`; by his own rule `yew` and `-êk` never combine, see §16.)

## 8. The linking particle (veqetandek)

Kurmanji has `-ê` (m), `-a` (f) and `-ên` (pl), and the adjective never changes. In Zazaki the linking particle depends on gender, number **and case**, and **the adjective agrees too** (§3.2, §12).

**Linking particle** (Lezgîn 2013, §3.2, §12.1–12.3)

| | noun + noun | noun + adjective, direct case | noun + adjective, oblique case |
|---|---|---|---|
| m | `Vengê Azadî` (dengê Azad) | `goliko sûr` (`-o`) | `golikê sûrî` (`-ê` ... `-î`) |
| f | `Beşna Asmîne` (bejna Asmînê) | `golika sûre` (`-a` ... `-e`) | `golika sûre` |
| pl | `Çimê Zelale` (çavên Zelalê) | `golikê sûrî` (`-ê` ... `-î`) | `golikanê sûran` (`-anê` ... `-an`) |
| Kurmanji | -ê / -a / -ên | golikê / golika / golikên sor | the same |

In sentences (§3.2):
- `Golikê sûrî bibere.` = Golikê sor bibe. (oblique, masculine)
- `Golika sûre bibere.` = Golika sor bibe.
- `Golikanê sûran bibere.` = Golikên sor bibe.

Watch these points:
- **Masculine direct uses `-o`, not `-ê`:** `daro derg`, `koyo sipî` 'white mountain', `nano patîle`. After a vowel it becomes `-yo`: `Cayo germin` 'a warm place', `Zamayo teze yo.` (§3.6, §5.2.1).
- **The feminine `-e` moves to the adjective:** `qeleme` + `sûr` → `qelema sûre` (§3.4).
- **Chains:** `Ehmedo dergo sûro qelew vano.` (direct) but `Ehmedê dergê sûrê qelewî vatbi.` (oblique). Kurmanji has `Ehmedê dirêjê sorê qelew` both times (§12.3).
- **Detached form:** `Lajê Mehemedî yo sûr ame.` = Lawê Mehemedî yê sor hat (§12.3).

## 9. Prepositions and postpositions

Kurmanji uses prepositions and circumpositions (`ji`, `li`, `di ... de`, `ji ... re`). **Zazaki mostly puts the word after the noun** (§8).

**Postpositions** (§8.2)

| Zazaki | Kurmanji | example |
|---|---|---|
| `X de` | di X de / li X | `Kitab de nuşte yo.` = Di kitêbê de nivîsandî ye. |
| `X ra` | ji X | `Ez Dîyarbekir ra yena.` = Ez ji Diyarbekirê têm. |
| `X ra` (with 'say') | ji X re | `Ez to ra vana.` = Ez ji te re dibêjim. |
| `X rê` | ji X re, ji bo X | `Ez to rê yew kitab herînena.` = Ez ji te re kitêbekê dikirim. |
| `X ro` | di X re, li X | `Uca ro meşo, tîya ro şo.` = Di wir re neçe, di vir re biçe. |
| `X ser o` | li ser X | `Ma to ser o qisey kerd.` = Me li ser te qise kir. |
| `X wa / ya / a` | bi X ve | `Camêrd çeka xo ya ameyo.` = Camêr bi çeka xwe ve hatiye. |

**Prepositions** (§8.1)

| Zazaki | Kurmanji | example |
|---|---|---|
| `bi` | bi; also 'to' | `Ez bi qeleme nusena.` = Ez bi qelemê dinivîsim. `Ez şina bi Dîyarbekir.` = Ez diçim Diyarbekirê. |
| `bê` | bê | `Ez bê to senî şina uca?` = Ez bê te çawa biçim wir? |
| `ro` | li | `Ez ro to gêrena.` = Ez li te digerim. |
| `ver bi` | ber bi | `Ehmed ver bi ma yeno.` = Ehmed ber bi me tê. |

**Circumpositions:**
- `di ... de` = di ... de;
- `bi ... ra` and `bi ... wa / ya` mean 'along with': `Manga bi golike ra biherîne.` = Çêlekê bi golikê ve bikire. (§8.3)

**"To love, to like"** is `X ra hes kerdene` = ji X hez kirin: `Ez Roşna ra hes kena.` = Ez ji Roşnayê hez dikim. (§4.3)

## 10. Word order and preverbs

Word order is SOV, as in Kurmanji, and Lezgîn says the two have the same syntax (§18). A verb of motion can come before its goal: `Ez şina [bi] Dîyarbekir.` = Ez diçim Diyarbekirê.

One real freedom Kurmanji lacks: **one-syllable preverbs can go before or after the verb**, and the meaning stays the same (§16.2.1).
- `Mi ber akerd.` = `Mi ber kerd a.` = Min derî vekir.
- `Mi kitab wedart.` = `Mi kitab dart we.` = Min kitêb hilanî.

The preverbs are `a-, ci-, de-, pa-, pê-, ra-, ro-, tê-, ta-, we-`. Two-syllable preverbs (`piro, pêro, pira, cêra`) do this less (§16.2.2).

## 15. Confidence and gaps

**What is solid.** These come straight from Lezgîn's tables, with a Kurmanji sentence for each:
- noun gender, number and case;
- the linking particle;
- present, past and future paradigms of `kewtene`, `kerdene`, `şîyene`, `werdene`, `vistene` and `dîyene`;
- the ergative system;
- the copula in the third person;
- negation and imperatives.

The book is built for Kurmanji speakers, so its examples are paired by design.

**Where the sources disagree** (we follow Lezgîn and note the variant):
- **1sg subjunctive and future:** `-î` in Lezgîn's tables (`Ez do binusî`) but `-a` in his own texts and in the E–Z sentences (`Ganî ez binusa`; `Mi waşt ke ez wina bikera`).
- **3sg feminine subjunctive:** both `bikewo` and `bikewa`, both `şiro` and `şira` (§17.3.1).
- **Gender of single words:**
  - `hewte` 'week' is masculine in one table and feminine in the next (§3.3);
  - `estare` 'star' is masculine in Lezgîn and feminine in Wîkîferheng;
  - `dewe` 'village' is feminine in Lezgîn's usage (`dewa ma`) and masculine in Wîkîferheng;
  - `estor` 'horse' is masculine in Wîkîferheng, but Lezgîn writes `bi estore`.
- **Lezgîn's own gender labels:** a few labels in his pronoun-object tables contradict the forms (for example `Mi o dî.` is labelled feminine). The forms themselves are consistent.
- **Lezgîn's Kurmanji is not Hawar standard.** He writes `dihê` for `tê`, `nîne` for the negative copula, and so on. All Kurmanji in this document is normalised to Hawar.

**What our sources do not show** (left empty in tables, never filled in):
- the past copula for `ti` and `şima`;
- a full positive copula paradigm for `şima` from Lezgîn (only `Şima kam ê?` from the E–Z sentences);
- several cells in the pronoun-object grids;
- the conditional and the irrealis beyond Lezgîn's tables;
- most of the Dêrsim forms;
- speaker numbers.

**What needs a native speaker:**
- the `-î / -a` 1sg subjunctive;
- whether male speakers everywhere use feminine agreement with `ez` (Lezgîn says yes, and the E–Z sentences agree: `Ez Kutik a` for a male character);
- the stress contrast in pairs like `gule` (f) and `gule` (m), which our text sources cannot show;
- the natural word for 'friend' (`embaz`) and 'big' (`gird` versus `pîl`) across regions.

**No native speaker has checked this document or the course built from it.**

## 16. Corrections found while building the course

- **§7.3 and §6.3:** Lezgîn's line is `Yew camêrd/camêrdêko derg ame.`, giving the two alternatives `Yew camêrd ... ame` and `Camêrdêko derg ame`. It does not give `Yew camêrdêko derg ame`. That combination breaks his own rule that `yew` and `-êk` never appear together. The course uses `Camêrdêko derg ame.` = Mêrekî dirêj hat.
- **§6.4, "you" as object:** the `dîyene` grid gives `Mi ti dî.` (to a man) and `Mi ti dîye.` (to a woman). The `vistene` grid gives `Mi ti vistî.` (to a man) and `Mi ti viste.` (to a woman). The two grids disagree on the masculine form, so the course does not teach "you" as an object in the past.
- **Simple past negative of `kewtene`:** Lezgîn gives it only with nouns (`Ehmed nêkewt`, `Asmîne nêkewte`, `Wendekarî nêkewtî`). The pronoun list `Ez nêkewta, Ti nêkewtê ...` belongs to the perfect. In the course table, the pronoun cells of the simple-past negative are marked as built, from `nê-` plus the attested positive form.
- **Review pass (spelling sources and word classes):**
  - The course sentence `Camêrdêko derg ame.` is marked as built in the data: it applies Lezgîn's rule to his slash line rather than copying a whole sentence.
  - `embaz`, `varan` and `kutik` appear bare in Wîkîferheng, and `xirab` and `qij` in the E–Z sentences; Lezgîn has them only with endings (`embazê`, `varanî`, `kutiko`, `xirabî`, `qijî`). The course cites the bare forms.
  - `kitab` is masculine; the course now shows it with `Kitabî bide Zelale` (§4.2). `Kitab de nuşte yo` does not show the gender of `kitab`, because the subject of `yo` is an unnamed "it".
  - Word classes changed after review: `manga` 'cow', `veng` 'voice', `ameyene` 'come' and `ardene` 'bring' are not cognates of `çêlek`, `deng`, `hatin` and `anîn`, so they are counted as different words. `kardî` 'knife' (kêr) and `serdin` 'cold' (sar) are counted as cognates. The shared share is now 130 of 191 words (68%).
